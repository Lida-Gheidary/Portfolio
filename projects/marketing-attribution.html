01Overview

I began with a concern about last-touch attribution: it assigns all order credit to the final eligible marketing interaction, although a customer may encounter several channels before ordering. I wanted to investigate how much that rule shapes the assessment of channel performance.

QUESTION: How does using last-touch attribution shape the assessment of marketing channel performance, and how does that assessment change under alternative models and lookback windows?

DATA: Four Adobe Customer Journey Analytics demo exports for May 1–31, 2026. The analysis retains all 16 channels, including digital advertising, messaging, search, and offline channels.

METHOD: Compare last-touch with linear attribution, which shares credit equally across eligible interactions, and Adobe’s algorithmic allocation. Examine order credit, rankings, and differences between 14-day and 30-day lookbacks.

TOOLS: Python, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, SQLite, and VS Code. The standalone study uses HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for interactive comparisons.

02Findings and Interpretation

Each percentage compares an alternative model with last-touch within the same lookback window.

  • Model choice can reverse the comparison. At 30 days, Paid Search receives 12.09% more algorithmic credit but 5.71% less linear credit than last-touch.
  • Credit change and rank describe different things. Email receives 43.90% less algorithmic credit at 30 days, yet remains first by attributed orders under all six settings.
  • The window can change the classification. SMS receives 7.10% less algorithmic credit at 14 days and 17.47% less at 30 days, crossing the study’s descriptive −10% boundary.

What the study suggests

These results suggest that attribution settings, baseline order volume, and consistency across settings are relevant considerations when interpreting reported channel performance. A change in allocated credit alone does not establish a change in channel effectiveness.

The evidence is limited to aggregate demo exports. Model totals agree within each window but differ between windows. The data do not contain journey-level records, advertising costs, or experiments, so the analysis cannot establish incremental contribution or support budget recommendations.

03How It Was Built with AI

The code was generated and revised with AI assistance. Claude Sonnet was used for the initial implementation, and ChatGPT-6.1 Sol was used to review and revise the project.

Describe→Generate→Run in VS Code→Review→Revise

I defined the analytical question, supplied the exports, and worked through the implementation in successive steps. I ran proposed code in VS Code, inspected the results, reported issues, and requested corrections before continuing.

The revision addressed percentage units, chart labels, correlation reporting, and the SQL revenue benchmark. It also refined the research narrative: the findings describe sensitivity to attribution settings, rather than assuming that last-touch had been proven to undervalue particular channels.
